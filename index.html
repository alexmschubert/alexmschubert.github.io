I develop machine learning methods to expand access to life-saving medical interventions and uncover new insights into disease mechanisms. My research spans the path from scientific discovery to clinical care: building AI models that identify patients missed by existing diagnostic tools, using these models to understand the physiology underlying disease, and translating them into effective, affordable interventions.

I developed an AI model that identifies patients at high risk of sudden cardiac death missed by conventional clinical criteria. In this work, I also discovered a previously undescribed ECG biomarker that led to a new hypothesis about the physiological mechanisms underlying sudden cardiac death. This work was published in Nature and recognized with the Academic Discovery of the Year Award from California Life Sciences. I also develop AI tools to expand access to healthcare beyond the clinic, including a handheld ECG model to screen for previously unrecognized heart attacks in rural India. I am now advancing both projects toward prospective evaluation: I am conducting a study of AI-guided screening for cardiac arrhythmias across Sweden, Taiwan, and the United States, and working with collaborators to evaluate community-based deployment in Tamil Nadu.

I am a PhD candidate in Computational Precision Health at UC Berkeley and UCSF, advised by Ziad Obermeyer and Ahmed Alaa. I was previously a fellow at the Eric and Wendy Schmidt Center at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard. Before starting my PhD, I worked at McKinsey & Company, advising pharmaceutical and healthcare clients. When I am not thinking about medical AI, you can usually find me on the walls of the nearest climbing gym.
